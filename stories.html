The need

Before the well, families here walked long distances to collect water from open sources, several times a day. The water was often contaminated, and illnesses like typhoid and diarrhoea were common.

2

How we decided to drill

1
Community requestThe community asks Rafiki for help, and our team visits to meet local leaders.
2
Need assessmentWe look at how far families walk for water and how safe their current source is.
3
Site selectionWe study the local geology and choose a spot close to the people who will use it. Typical wells reach 100 to 400 feet.
4
Permits and financingPermits are secured and funding is confirmed before drilling begins.
5
Community commitmentA well committee agrees to look after, protect and maintain the well.
Decision: approved for drilling.
3

The drilling

Our drilling crew, or a trusted local drilling partner, works side by side with the community until they reach clean water.

The team then installs the pump ({{d.pumpLower}}) and caps the well with cement to keep the water safe.

4

After the well

Today the community draws safe water close to home, with fewer waterborne illnesses and more time for school, work and family.

Water is not flowing here yet. After photos and the community’s story will be added once the well is finished.
